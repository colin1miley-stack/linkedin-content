## Newsletter / Email Version

**Subject:** The single-threaded deal is one reorg away from dead

I lost a €47k deal last year because my champion got reorged to a different team.

The new person didn't know me. Didn't care. The deal died in three weeks without anyone saying no.

That's the single-thread trap. One contact. One relationship. One point of failure. And it's the most expensive habit in B2B sales.

**The numbers don't lie**

Gong Labs analyzed 1.8 million B2B deals closed in 2024. Multi-threaded deals — where you actively engage multiple stakeholders throughout the process — had a 130% win-rate lift over single-threaded ones.

Single-threaded win rate: 20%.
Multi-threaded: 42%.

The gap isn't your pitch. It's not your pricing. It's not even your product. It's your contact map.

Gartner's latest research puts the average B2B buying group at 6 to 10 decision-makers. If your entire deal rests on one person carrying your message to the other nine, you're not selling. You're hoping.

**What multi-threading actually looks like**

I used to think multi-threading meant "cc more people." It's not. It's building direct relationships with distinct roles across the buying committee.

Here's my current playbook:

**Call 1 — Map the committee**
"Who else needs to be in the room for this to move?"
"What's the approval process look like on your side?"
"Who could kill this deal, and what would they want to see?"

I write down every name. Then I research each one before the next touch.

**Call 2 — Bring in the economic buyer**
No proxies. No "I'll ask my manager." I get the VP or CFO on a separate 20-minute call focused on ROI, payback period, and risk.

**Between calls — Stakeholder-specific updates**
The CFO gets a one-page business case. The champion gets a progress email they can forward. The end user gets a workflow walkthrough.

Same deal. Different story for each person. Because a CFO doesn't care about your UI, and an end user doesn't care about your payback period.

**What I see auditing Irish B2B teams**

Most teams I work with have 60-70% of deals single-threaded. The rep is proud of their "strong relationship" with the champion. Meanwhile the deal has been in "proposal sent" for six weeks because procurement hasn't even seen the pricing yet.

The rep thinks they're managing one relationship well. They're managing one point of failure.

**Your action for this week**

Pull your top 5 open deals. Count the active buyer-side contacts in each one.

If any deal has fewer than 3 contacts with clear roles tagged in your CRM, that's your Monday job. Not more demos. Not more follow-up emails. More relationships.

Three contacts minimum. Champion, economic buyer, and one more. Tag them. Engage them directly. Make sure each one knows why this matters to them specifically.

The deals that survive reorgs, budget freezes, and priority shifts are the ones with multiple people inside the account who want them to close.

One champion isn't a deal. It's a foothold.

Talk next Friday,
Colin

P.S. — If your CRM doesn't have a "contacts per deal" field, create one this week. Most CRMs don't track this by default, which is why most teams don't see the problem until it's too late.
